I am a postdoctoral researcher with a deep passion for advancing artificial intelligence, particularly in the areas of deep learning architectures, AI validation, and uncertainty quantification. My research focuses on designing and implementing innovative machine learning models to tackle complex real-world challenges.

### Research interest
My work has involved:
- AI Model Development: Creating and deploying advanced architectures such as CNNs, LSTMs, Transformers, and Vision Transformers to analyze diverse datasets, from weather forecasts to sleep staging data.
- Deep Learning Applications: Applying state-of-the-art neural network designs for tasks like automatic sleep analysis and satellite-based environmental predictions.
- Uncertainty Quantification: Developing statistical methods and probabilistic models to assess and interpret uncertainty in AI predictions, enhancing model robustness and reliability.
- State-of-the-art in deep learning architecture: Exploring novel models like Kolmogorov MLP and XNet with Cauchy activation functions to enhance learning efficiency, model application and benchmarking.

With a foundation in applied mathematics and hands-on experience in industrial and academic projects, I aim to contribute to the development of trustworthy AI systems that integrate precision, adaptability, and transparency.

### Paper of interest

This year, I have come across several fascinating papers that have significantly contributed to the field of AI and deep learning. Here are a few that stood out:

- **["Probabilistic weather forecasting with machine learning"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08252-9)***: This study proposes a novel approach to provide weather forecast using the most advanded machine learning based weather prediction (MLWP) named GenCast, demonstrating significant improvements in ensemble weather forecast calibration. The core of the model lies on a conditional diffusion model, a generative ML method that can model the probability distribution of complex data.
- **["Cauchy activation function and XNet"](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.19221v1)**: This paper introduces a novel activation function named Cauchy activation challenging the performance of the traditional Relu. Cauchy activation works as an addition of two main terms that perform local approximation.
